Each local master is a decoded copy of Freesound's public HQ MP3 preview, not the original
lossless upload (the originals need a Freesound login). source.json records each download
URL, the hashes, the conversion and the edit regions; tools/generate_farming_audio.py rebuilds
the game's cues from these masters without a download.

dig.wav
Author: maxthrower
Source: https://freesound.org/people/maxthrower/sounds/168810/
Preview: https://cdn.freesound.org/previews/168/168810_3063236-hq.mp3
Local master: mattock_garden.wav
Used for: the copper hoe biting the ground (foley: a mattock digging a garden, not a hoe)
The uploader's description: "the sound of the garden being dug with a mattock. nice and chunky. can serve as an sfx in many digging scenarios.". The page states no processing. Found by matching the file against itself: its stretch from 3.6 s to 22.0 s is heard a second time from 22.2 s to 40.6 s, an edit the page does not mention. Every cue is cut from the first copy or from the part after 40.6 s that is not repeated, so each is its own recorded stroke.
Original upload: Wave (.wav), 44.1 kHz, 16 bit, Stereo; downloading it needs a Freesound login
Channel used: left (both channels are the same)

Soil Brush
Author: NeoSpica
Source: https://freesound.org/people/NeoSpica/sounds/426175/
Preview: https://cdn.freesound.org/previews/426/426175_7704891-hq.mp3
Local master: soil_brushed_by_hand.wav
Used for: a tile sown by hand (foley: a hand brushing the ground; no seed is heard)
The uploader's description: "Brushing the ground with my hand, recorded with a Zoom H1.". Tagged dirt, earth, forest, ground, soil. A quiet recording: above 150 Hz its strokes stand 16 to 18 dB over its own hiss. The page states no processing.

Channel used: right microphone (the stronger of two that differ)

Substitutes, named as such: the hoe is a mattock digging a garden, not a hoe. Three of the four
pulls are a weed being torn, not a crop pulled; the fourth is a plant pulled up out of the ground,
as in the game. The sowing is a hand brushing the ground, with no seed in it. No synthetic layers,
time stretching or pitch changes are baked into any cue.
